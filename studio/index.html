Not another black cylinder.

Every smart speaker looks the same: a plastic puck, a fabric cylinder, a little computer apologizing for existing. People have kept maneki-neko on their shelves for centuries - in shops, in kitchens, by front doors - because a lucky cat is a charm, not an appliance.

So that is where the computer goes. Lucky Cat is a voice assistant you would keep on the shelf even if it never said a word.

Its own brain, in your house.

The first prototype runs on a Raspberry Pi 5 with a working Gemini voice loop. Local wake word, speech, and the fine-tuned Qwen model are the target stack and are still in development.

  • Wake wordPush-to-talk now; local wake word next
  • BrainGemini now; fine-tuned local model in testing
  • VoiceWorking speaker loop; local TTS planned
  • BodyCeramic cat, electronics in the base
  • SoftwareOpen stack, built in public
  • HomeMade in Chicago
